Research

My Ph.D. research primarily revolves around nanomaterial synthesis and nanoelectronics fabrication, including low-dimensional materials for transistors and memory devices, GaN-based LED arrays for optogenetics research with neural cells, and solution-processed electronics for fundamental logic computations. This focus has solidified my expertise in the fundamentals of semiconductor device physics across a wide range of components.

My research experience has made me a specialist in experimental work, with deep understanding of and hands-on skill in semiconductor fabrication processes. I am also proficient in materials characterization and electronic device measurements, and I pick up and master new techniques quickly — an ability I bring to both research and engineering settings.

Explore my selected Ph.D. projects below.

Atomically Thin Amorphous Carbon Films Synthesized from Solution Precursor

A scalable, solution-based strategy to prepare large-area, freestanding, atomically thin amorphous carbon films as novel dielectrics for nanoelectronics — including 2D transistors and memristors with unprecedented performance and uniformity.

Learn more →

Solution-Processable Ordered Defect Compound Semiconductors for High-Performance Electronics

New solution-based approaches to deposit chalcogenide electronic materials — mainly CuIn5Se8 — for high-performance thin-film transistors, with much faster throughput and lower equipment cost than vacuum-based deposition.

Learn more →

CMOS-Compatible and Scalable Electrochemical Synaptic Transistor Arrays for Deep-Learning Accelerator

An electrochemical synaptic transistor built with CMOS-compatible metal oxides, operating by shuffling protons within a symmetric gate stack — delivering high-speed, symmetric analog programming with small variability, scalable to nanometer footprints.

Learn more →

Synthetic Neurocomputers for Cognitive Information Processing

A neurocomputer prototype that uses biological neuronal circuits, engineered into well-defined 3D topologies reminiscent of deep-neural-network models, as its information-processing units — with integrated electronic and optoelectronic devices to drive and monitor each neural cell.
